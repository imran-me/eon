EON — One Brain, Multi Decision Layer

A decision layer that sits on top of the Epal ERP and answers the way a chief of staff would
Concept note
Epal Group · 12 companies
eon.gulfrabit.com

An ERP records what happened. It does not tell you what it means, what it costs you, or what to do next. EON is the layer that does — ask it in Bangla or English, by voice or text, and it answers from the live ledger with the number, the rule that produced it, and the action it recommends. Then, if you say yes, it carries that action out through the ERP's own screens.

The gap it closes

The Epal ERP holds 203 tables across 293 screens — accounts, payroll, HR, sales, CRM, tasks, attendance. Every figure a director needs is already in there. What is missing is the step between a figure and a decision:

  • No screen prints why profit fell, only that it did.
  • No report says which of twelve companies is burning money, or qualifies its own answer when the data is incomplete.
  • Nothing audits the books and volunteers what nobody reported.
  • Nothing speaks Bangla, and the boss thinks in Bangla.

What EON delivers

  • Ask — any question, either language, voice or text, grounded in live data
  • Brief — a ranked morning briefing, built before anyone opens a screen
  • Decide — approvals queued, overdue items surfaced, anomalies named
  • Act — pay, assign, message, notify — through the ERP's own forms
  • Explain — the rule behind the number, in the language asked

How it was built

EON was not started from a blank page. Three working systems were merged into one companion core, and the enterprise brain was built on top of it.

OriginContributed
OppTrackerThe application shell — tasks, documents, contacts, ledger, work sheet, the responsive pass
Personal OSThe brain — intelligence deck, natural-language agent, finance coach, prediction, self-correction
EON for TeacherProof it runs fully offline — no CDN, no external dependency, vendored engine

None of the three shared history, so the merge was done as a real three-way merge against the fork point, then extended: a pluggable domain registry so every space answers the same way, an ERP knowledge domain, a PHP server beside the ERP, voice, and the command centre.

It rides on the ERP without touching it

One script tag is appended to each ERP page by an output-buffer callback in PHP. Not a single line of ERP source is edited — no migration, no fork, no merge conflict at the next ERP upgrade. Delete two files and the ERP is exactly what it was. Deployment is git push; the site is live in about sixty seconds.

The path of a question

AskVoice or text, Bangla, English or Banglish
UnderstandIntent and language detected — 52 intents, or read structurally
AnswerLive ledger read, rule applied, figure decomposed
PhraseWritten natively in the language asked
SpeakRendered as speech, server-side

The whole round trip lands in 15–60 milliseconds. Nothing is queued, nothing is sent anywhere, and the answer is reproducible because it is arithmetic over the ledger, not a guess.

Grounded in the real system, not a sample of it

EON reads the production database directly through a read-only path — 54 sections covering the chart of accounts, journals and ledgers, payroll and payslips, loans and advances, attendance, expenses, sales and invoices, party transactions, bank transfers, petty cash, and the service business the group actually runs.

It also knows the ERP as software. The application was mapped once and that map is part of its knowledge, so "where do I find payslips" is answered with the real screen and the real path.

It reasons, it does not retrieve

Asked "why", EON decomposes a figure until it reaches something a person can act on: which of income, direct cost or overhead actually moved; whether overheads exceed income outright; how much of the overhead is payroll; what is still unapproved and therefore absent from the number; and, for cash, whether collecting every overdue taka would even close the gap.

"Why is cash so tight?"
Cash ৳1.89 L against a burn of ৳2.17 L a month — about 0.9 months, and 99% of that burn is payroll. ৳15.5 L is owed to you and all of it is past due. Against that you owe ৳20.7 L. So the gap is ৳17.8 L, and collecting every overdue taka would still not close it.

That last sentence is what a director needs, and no report prints it.

Two languages, natively

Bangla is not a translation layer bolted on the end — the reasoning runs in both languages and every answer is composed in the language it was asked in. Money is spoken on the Bangladeshi scale (লাখ, কোটি), Bengali numerals are read as numerals, account codes are read digit by digit, and menu paths and URLs are never read aloud. Where a machine has no Bangla voice installed — most machines — the server renders the speech itself, so Bangla works on any device with nothing to install.

It acts, under control

An instruction becomes a proposal, the proposal is confirmed, and the confirmed action is posted to the ERP's own route and its own form — the same endpoint the page's own button uses. Money and deletions always ask. A half-filled form stops rather than guessing. A response of "success" is not taken at face value: a refusal is read and reported as a refusal.

A question passes only if it lands on the right intent, avoids the fallback, and answers in the language it was asked in. Every runner prints the holes rather than a grade, and adding a phrasing moves the denominator by itself.

Security is inherited, not invented

EON trusts the ERP's own session — signed in to the ERP means signed in to EON, on the same origin, with nothing to paste. Endpoints are fail-closed: no session, no data. The database user is read-only for analysis; every write goes back through the ERP's own permission checks, so a user can never ask EON for something the ERP would refuse them.

What it found in its first month

The clearest case for EON is not a feature list. Reading the live books unprompted, it reported four things nobody had raised — none of them software faults, all of them money:

FindingConsequence
৳5.27 L of a month's sales never reached the ledger — the desks invoiced ৳9.12 L, the journal carries ৳3.85 LEvery profit figure understates the business by more than half
23 of 80 payslips do not add up on their own numbers — the worst is ৳30,000 out on one slipDirect payroll exposure, repeating monthly
Advances are not applied to invoices — one customer paid ৳9.98 L and still shows ৳8.5 L dueCollections chasing money already banked
The stored running balance is written in entry order, not date orderAny back-dated entry leaves the ledger disagreeing with itself

EON reports both figures where they conflict and names the gap, rather than quietly picking one. That single habit — qualifying its own answer — is the difference between a dashboard and an adviser.

Where the value sits in a business

Time

A question that took an accountant an afternoon of exports is answered in under a second, without opening a screen.

Leakage

Unjournalised sales, mis-computed payslips, unapplied advances and stale balances are found continuously, not at audit.

Reach

The owner does not need to read English or navigate 293 screens to run twelve companies. He asks.

The decision that defines it: EON ships with no language model connected. Everything above is produced by rules, grammar and analytics over the real database. That is not a limitation worked around — it is why the answers are fast, free to run, auditable, private, and identical every time they are asked. A model can be added later for phrasing; it is not needed for truth.

Where it goes

Portability

The integration surface is one adapter and one domain registry. A different ERP means a new adapter and a new knowledge map — the brain, the language layer, the voice, the reasoning and the action pipeline are unchanged. Every mid-market ERP install in the country has the same gap between its data and its decisions.

Economics

Because there is no per-query cost, the pricing can be per-installation rather than per-question, and the marginal cost of an extra user, an extra company or a heavier day is effectively zero. Deployment is a clone beside the customer's existing system, not a migration off it.
