EON — One Brain, Multi Decision Layer

A decision layer on top of the Epal ERP that answers the way a chief of staff would
Concept note
Epal Group · 12 companies
eon.gulfrabit.com

An ERP records what happened. It does not tell you what it means, what it is costing you, or what to do next. EON is the layer that does — ask in Bangla or English, by voice or text, and it answers from the live ledger with the number, the rule that produced it, and the action it recommends. Say yes, and it carries that action out through the ERP's own screens.

The gap it closes

The ERP holds 203 tables across 293 screens — accounts, payroll, HR, sales, CRM, tasks, attendance. Every figure a director needs is already in there. What is missing is the step between a figure and a decision:

  • No screen prints why profit fell, only that it did.
  • No report ranks twelve companies, or qualifies itself when the data is incomplete.
  • Nothing audits the books and volunteers what nobody reported.
  • Nothing speaks Bangla, and the owner thinks in Bangla.

What EON delivers

  • Ask — any question, either language, voice or text, grounded in live data
  • Brief — a ranked morning briefing, built before anyone opens a screen
  • Decide — approvals queued, overdue items surfaced, anomalies named
  • Act — pay, assign, message, notify, through the ERP's own forms
  • Explain — the rule behind the number, in the language asked

How it was built

EON did not start from a blank page. Three working systems were merged into one companion core — a real three-way merge against the fork point, not a copy — and the enterprise brain was built on top.

OriginContributed
OppTrackerThe application shell — tasks, documents, contacts, ledger, work sheet
Personal OSThe brain — natural-language agent, finance coach, prediction, self-correction
EON for TeacherProof it runs fully offline — no CDN, no external dependency

On that base: a pluggable domain registry so every space answers the same way, an ERP knowledge domain, a PHP server beside the ERP, voice, and the command centre.

It rides on the ERP without touching it

One script tag is appended to each ERP page by an output-buffer callback in PHP. Not a single line of ERP source is edited — no fork, no migration, no merge conflict at the next upgrade. Delete two files and the ERP is exactly what it was. Deployment is a git push — the site is live in about sixty seconds.

Security is inherited the same way. Signed in to the ERP means signed in to EON — same origin, nothing to paste. Endpoints are fail-closed, the database user is read-only, and every write goes back through the ERP's own permission checks: no one can ask EON for what the ERP would refuse them.

It reasons, it does not retrieve

Asked "why", EON decomposes a figure until it reaches something a person can act on: which of income, direct cost or overhead moved; how much of the overhead is payroll; what is unapproved and therefore absent from the number; and whether collecting every overdue taka would close the gap.

"Why is cash so tight?"
Cash ৳1.89 L against a burn of ৳2.17 L a month — about 0.9 months, and 99% of that burn is payroll. ৳15.5 L is owed to you and all of it is past due. Against that you owe ৳20.7 L. So the gap is ৳17.8 L, and collecting every overdue taka would still not close it.

That last sentence is what a director needs; no report prints it.

Two languages, natively

Bangla is not a translation bolted on the end — the reasoning runs in both languages and every answer is composed in the language it was asked in, with money spoken on the Bangladeshi scale (লাখ, কোটি). Where a machine has no Bangla voice — most machines — the server renders the speech itself.

It acts, under control

An instruction becomes a proposal; the confirmed proposal is posted to the ERP's own route and its own form — the endpoint the page's own button uses. Money and deletions always ask, a half-filled form stops rather than guessing, and a refusal is read and reported as a refusal.

What it found in its first month

Reading the live books unprompted, EON reported four things nobody had raised — none of them software faults, all of them money:

FindingConsequence
৳5.27 L of a month's sales never reached the ledgerEvery profit figure understates the business by more than half
23 of 80 payslips do not add up — the worst is ৳30,000 outDirect payroll exposure, repeating monthly
Advances not applied — a customer paid ৳9.98 L, still shows ৳8.5 L dueCollections chasing money already banked
Running balances stored in entry order, not date orderA back-dated entry leaves the ledger disagreeing with itself

Where two figures conflict EON reports both and names the gap rather than quietly picking one — and that habit, qualifying its own answer, is the difference between a dashboard and an adviser.

Time

A question that took an accountant an afternoon of exports is answered in under a second.

Wrong payslips and unapplied advances are found continuously, not at audit.

The decision that defines it: EON ships with no language model connected — everything above is rules, grammar and analytics over the real database. That is not a limitation worked around; it is why the answers are fast, free to run, auditable, private and identical every time. A model can be added later for phrasing; it is not needed for truth.

Where it goes

Portability

The integration surface is one adapter and one knowledge map. A different ERP needs both rewritten; the brain, the language layer, the voice and the action pipeline are unchanged. Every mid-market ERP has the same gap between its data and its decisions.

Economics

With no per-query cost, pricing can be per installation rather than per question, and the marginal cost of another user or a heavier day is effectively zero. Deployment is a clone beside the existing system, not a migration off it.
